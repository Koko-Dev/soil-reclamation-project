The Soil Revival Project

No More Lawns. No More Lies. Heal the Ground.

Why This Plan Exists

It now takes three carrots to give you the same nutrition as one carrot in 1940. Why? Because the soil is dead. Residential development, chemical treatments, and lifeless grass lawns have stripped it of minerals, microbes, and meaning.

This roadmap isn't just for Hampton — it's for anywhere the ground has been forgotten. It's a 7-year plan to rebuild the soil beneath our feet. It doesn't require perfection. It requires action.

The 7-Year Restoration Roadmap

Year 1 – Assessment & Ground Preparation

  • Test soil (pH, compaction, nutrients)
  • Loosen soil (broadfork or shallow tilling)
  • Add compost
  • Sow: White Clover, Buckwheat, Hairy Vetch
  • Mulch to hold moisture and suppress weeds

Year 2 – Root Penetration & Microbial Activity

  • Repeat cover crops for biomass
  • Use compost or aged manure
  • No chemicals. No exceptions.
  • Look for worms, softer soil, less runoff

Year 3 – Nutrient Cycling & Weed Suppression

  • Deeper-rooting plants: Yarrow, Comfrey, Deep Clover
  • Mow & mulch in place
  • Weeds decline, moisture retention rises

Year 4 – Soil Structure & Biological Diversity

  • Rotate crops by root depth
  • Add fungi (optional)
  • Crumbly texture = success

Year 5 – Topsoil Gains & Taste Returns

  • Test for 4–6% organic matter
  • Spot plant: Borage, Daikon
  • Perennials for year-round health

Year 6 – Consistency & Confidence

  • Alternate warm/cool cover crops
  • Treat weak zones, not the whole yard
  • Grow real food if desired

Year 7 – Audit & Spread the Restoration

  • Retest everything
  • Write a maintenance plan
  • Help your neighbor start theirs
